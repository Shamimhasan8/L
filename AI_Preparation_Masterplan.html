01 · the senior mindset

What separates an expert from a junior

At the senior and staff levels, companies are not just looking for people who can build an AI feature; they want engineers who understand production lifecycles, cost-optimization, trade-offs, and failure modes.

  • Trade-off Fluency over Rote Implementation: Instead of asking "What is RAG?", interviewers will ask "When would you NOT use RAG?" You must be fluent in the trade-offs between retrieval speed vs. context length, fine-tuning vs. prompting, and GPU cost vs. latency.
  • Cost & Latency Reasoning: You must think in terms of token budgets and per-query costs. (e.g., "100K daily users x 10 interactions x ~2K tokens = 2B tokens/day = $13K/day on GPT-4 Turbo").
  • Systems Thinking: Shift your perspective from building pipelines to managing lifecycles. Generative AI system design requires thinking in loops: retrieval, generation, evaluation, and feedback.
Honest Uncertainty: Knowing what you don't know and admitting it ("I need a hint" or "I haven't used that tool, but...") is a feature of real production experience. It outperforms bluffing every time.
02 · 12-week roadmap

The Preparation Masterplan

Based on patterns from 100+ candidates, hiring managers, and top-tier AI labs. This is the timeline.

P1
Weeks 1-3: Deep Conceptual Foundations
Solidify algorithmic intuition and math foundations.
▼
  • Data Structures & Algorithms: Complete NeetCode 250 using spaced repetition. Focus on the problem-solving intuition rather than memorization.
  • Deep Python Mastery: Be prepared to discuss race conditions, the Global Interpreter Lock (GIL), async patterns, and concurrency vs. parallelism.
  • ML Mechanics from Scratch: Be able to implement Multi-Head Attention, Transformer layers, LoRA, and a KV cache from memory using NumPy or PyTorch.
  • Theory: Read Simon Prince's "Understanding Deep Learning".
P2
Weeks 4-6: Production Architecture & System Design
Transition to web-scale, resilient AI systems.
▼
  • Traditional System Design: Read Alex Xu's "System Design Interview" and skim DDIA.
  • GenAI Patterns: Master the 7 core patterns: evals, RAG, fine-tuning, caching, guardrails, defensive UX, and the data flywheel.
  • Agentic AI Design: Explain orchestrator vs. LLM responsibility splits and agent termination conditions.
P3
Weeks 7-8: Evaluation, Observability & Safety
Mastering real-world model deployment constraints.
▼
  • Evaluation Frameworks: Learn Ragas, DeepEval, or LLM-as-judge. Every RAG system must have an eval harness.
  • Metrics: Track TTFT (Time to First Token), TBT (Time Between Tokens), and hallucination drift.
  • Safety: Master protections against prompt injection, data leakage, and unsafe tool execution.
P4
Weeks 9-10: High-Impact Portfolio & Take-Homes
Prove capability through metric-driven projects.
▼
  • End-to-End Projects: Build 2-3 polished, deployed projects with Docker, CI/CD, and monitoring.
  • Focus on Impact: Frame achievements in metrics. "Reduced support response time by 40%" beats "Experience with LangChain".
  • Take-Home Excellence: Document decisions, test edge cases, and submit code alongside a Loom architectural video.
P5
Weeks 11-12: Storytelling & Interview Polish
Secure the optimal offer.
▼
  • The SAIL Framework: Situation, Action, Impact, Learning. Map stories explicitly to target company values.
  • Verbalize Trade-offs: Practice explaining complex trade-offs aloud without hesitation.
  • Strategic Networking: Reach out directly to founders via LinkedIn. Referrals bypass crowded ATS systems.
03 · critical mistakes

Common pitfalls in the interview

Avoid these traps that signal a lack of production experience to hiring managers.

Jumping to Fine-tuning: Default to prompt engineering and hybrid RAG. Only suggest fine-tuning if extreme specialization or latency strictness demands it.
Name-dropping without Trade-offs: If you say "I'd use LangChain" or "I'd use Redis," you must explain why and know exactly when it is the wrong choice.
Treating the LLM as Truth: Always ground model outputs with retrieval, tools, or citations.
The Anti-Mistake: Over-engineering. Start by getting a working implementation, discuss the simplest MVP, then optimize on the follow-ups.
04 · job search strategy

Negotiation & Job Search Tactics

  • Market Categorization: Focus your search on one of three segments: Product companies (Cursor), Infrastructure companies (Pinecone), or Model companies (OpenAI).
  • Compensation Benchmarking: Always benchmark by Total Compensation (Base + Equity + Bonuses). AI engineers command a 10-20% premium.
  • Leverage Equity: Direct your negotiation leverage toward equity grant sizes, as base salary bands are usually rigid. Your strongest negotiation move is always a competing offer.
  • Startup Due Diligence: If moving to a startup, act like an investor. Evaluate revenue growth and market size. Red flag: Refusal to share financials post-offer.
  • Move Quickly: Top candidates accept offers within 2-3 weeks. Cluster your onsites together so you can negotiate competing offers simultaneously.
